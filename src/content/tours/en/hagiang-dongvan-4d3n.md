Ha Giang is a trip where the road matters as much as the destinations. Most of the four days is spent in a vehicle following a cliff edge, and that is exactly what you are paying to see: a stone plateau running to the horizon, maize planted on slopes too steep to stand on, and the Happiness Road that thousands of volunteers cut with hammers and crowbars between 1959 and 1965.

We use a smaller vehicle than usual on this route. The road up to Dong Van has tight hairpins and stretches barely a lane wide; a full-size coach is both slower and unable to pull over at the best viewpoints. We also leave early each morning, because after ten o'clock the cloud usually rises and fills the valleys.

This trip suits travellers who cope well with long drives and winding roads. Buckwheat flowers are out around October and November, plum and peach blossom around February and March. If you want a specific season, tell us early — Dong Van has very few hotel rooms and they go quickly. Group size and accommodation standard are agreed before the contract is signed.
