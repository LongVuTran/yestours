Da Lat is the one place in Vietnam where visitors put a jacket on in the evening in the middle of summer. The city sits at around 1,500 metres on the Lam Vien plateau; the French built it in the early twentieth century as somewhere to escape the heat, and it still has the steep-roofed villas, the pine slopes and a pace noticeably slower than the lowlands.

We keep the programme short — three places a day. A lot of what has opened in Da Lat over the past few years exists purely to be photographed, and chasing all of it means spending the day in the coach. What is in this itinerary are the places that still have a reason to exist: a railway station from 1938, a tea estate from 1927, and a mountain the K'Ho people still tell stories about.

The Cau Dat morning is deliberately on the last day, because that is when the mist is still sitting on the tea rows. The drive out of town runs past vegetable plots and greenhouses, which shows you what Da Lat actually lives on. The trip works well for families with children. Hotel category and group size are agreed before the contract is signed.
