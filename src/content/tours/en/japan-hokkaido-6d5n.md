Hokkaido is not like the rest of Japan: a big island, thinly populated, with straight roads and few traffic lights. That means more time on the coach than on a Tokyo–Osaka route, and in exchange, long stretches of road through farmland with no buildings in sight — something you rarely get in Japan.

We keep one ryokan night with an onsen in the itinerary. For many travellers this is their first public hot spring, so the tour leader goes through the rules before you go in: wash thoroughly before entering the water, never take a towel into the bath, and tell us in advance if you have tattoos so we can book somewhere that accommodates them.

Season matters more here than on most trips. The flowers at Biei and Furano peak between June and August; December to February is deep snow, with the Sapporo Snow Festival in early February. We prepare and submit the Japanese visa applications on your behalf; you supply a valid passport and the documents on a checklist we send in advance. Group size, accommodation standard and departure dates are fixed when the contract is signed.
